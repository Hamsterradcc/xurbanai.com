Entry standard · v1

What it takes to enter a registry.

One standard, three registries. The common fields apply to everything; each registry adds the fields its kind of work needs. Missing fields block a release.

How the standard is used

Entry cards are stored as JSON in the repository and rendered on the registry pages. Changes to the standard are versioned and recorded in the repository history.

Common fields

Required for every entry.

Urban question
The city problem the work addresses, and who is meant to use the result.
Data provenance
Sources, geography, time span, licence, and processing steps for every dataset used.
Evaluation
Tasks, metrics, baselines, and protocol, with results reported as released.
Failure modes
Known limitations, observed errors, and conditions under which the result should not be trusted.
Geographic validity
Cities tested, and whether claims are local or supported by cross-city evidence.
Artifacts & citation
Code, configurations, weights or task files, a version, a licence, and how to cite the entry.
Registry 01

Model fields.

Intended taskThe prediction, representation, or generation task, and the urban decision it is meant to inform.
Inputs & modalitiesImagery, maps, mobility traces, text, tabular records, or sensor streams, with resolution and preprocessing.
Training dataSources, geography, time span, licence, sampling, and any data excluded and why.
EvaluationHeld-out tasks, metrics, baselines, and uncertainty, with evaluation code released.
Failure modesObserved errors, subgroup gaps, distribution shifts, and conditions under which outputs should not be used.
Usage constraintsIntended users, prohibited uses, and requirements for human review.
ArtifactsCode, configuration, weights or access route, version, licence, and citation.
Registry 02

Agent fields.

Task & workflowThe research or planning task, the steps performed, and where the workflow starts and stops.
Tools & data accessModels, datasets, APIs, and documents the agent can call or read, with their licences.
Decision boundariesWhat the agent may decide alone, what requires a human, and how outputs are reviewed.
Evaluation protocolSuccess criteria, reliability across runs, comparison with human performance, and reproducibility.
Failure modesObserved errors, fabrication risks, bias in retrieved evidence, and misuse scenarios.
TraceabilityLogs, intermediate outputs, and citations produced so that results can be audited.
ArtifactsCode, prompts or policies, configuration, version, licence, and citation.
Registry 03

Benchmark fields.

Research questionThe urban capability the task measures, and why it matters for a decision.
Task definitionInputs, expected outputs, and the exact instructions or prompts given to a system under test.
DataSource, geography, time span, licence, splits, and how test data is protected from leakage.
Metrics & criteriaScoring rules, what counts as success, and how uncertainty or disagreement is reported.
BaselinesReference systems, including simple and human baselines where they exist, with scores.
Submission rulesHow results are submitted, what must be disclosed, and how evaluation code is run.
Generalization protocolHow performance is tested across cities, periods, or populations beyond the development data.
Versioning & errataVersion history, corrections to data or scoring, and how earlier results are handled.
Release checklist

Before an entry goes live.

Card completeEvery required field is filled. “To follow” is not an accepted value.
Evaluation reproducibleEvaluation code runs from the released artifacts and reproduces the reported numbers.
Licences checkedData and code licences permit the release, and restricted data is described rather than redistributed.
Version assignedThe entry carries a version, a release date, and a citation; later changes get a new version.
Status & versioning

How entries change over time.

draftCard in preparation. Stored in the repository but not shown on the registry pages.
releasedCard complete and artifacts public. Shown on the registry page with its version.
deprecatedSuperseded or withdrawn. Remains listed for the record, with a pointer to its replacement where one exists.
VersionsSemantic versions. A change to data, scoring, weights, or task definition increments the version; results are always reported against a version.
Out of scope

What is not accepted.

Demos without evaluationInterfaces or outputs with no stated task, metric, or baseline.
Unverifiable claimsResults on data that cannot be described well enough for an independent check.
Undocumented transferClaims of generality without evidence beyond the development city.
Restricted materialData, weights, or documents the submitter does not have the right to release.
